## Overview

Transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation (tSCS) can activate motor pools below the level of a spinal cord injury without surgery, making it an appealing route to restoring volitional movement. What remains unclear is **where** on the spine to stimulate and **when** within a movement to deliver that stimulation in order to recruit a specific muscle group selectively.

This ongoing project at the Seanez Lab (Washington University in St. Louis) investigates the spatiotemporal parameters of multielectrode tSCS arrays in people with chronic spinal cord injury, with the goal of increasing targeted joint torque while limiting off-target recruitment.

## Approach

**Spatial optimization.** We evaluate multielectrode tSCS arrays across participants, systematically varying array positioning to identify the configurations that maximize torque at a targeted joint.

**Probing spinal excitability.** Custom frequency-modulated stimulation software evaluates posterior root-muscle (PRM) reflexes through post-activation depression, which distinguishes genuine reflex responses from direct motor-axon activation. Autonomic nociception and participant pain profiles are monitored in real time throughout.

**Synchronized multi-modal recording.** A 16-channel wireless EMG system and a Biodex isokinetic dynamometer are integrated so that high-density neurophysiological signals, joint torque, and 3D kinematics are captured on a common time base.

**Statistical design.** Outcomes are analyzed with linear mixed-effects models within a 15-session crossover design, which accommodates the repeated-measures structure of the protocol and the variability across participants.

## Scope

The study protocol runs across **16 chronic spinal cord injury participants**. Outcome measures include targeted joint torque, muscle recruitment selectivity, and PRM reflex characteristics.

## Status

Data collection and analysis are ongoing. Results will be posted here as the work is published.
