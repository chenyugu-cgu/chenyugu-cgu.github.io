## Accepted to ISMR 2026 — Best Student Paper Award Finalist.

# Abstract
Minimally invasive surgery requires steerable tools that can navigate tortuous anatomy without trauma, yet actuating continuum robots at the millimeter scale remains challenging. This work presents a hydraulically actuated, polymer-based continuum robot with an outer diameter of just 2.14 mm, fabricated from MRI-compatible, non-conductive materials. A custom coiled hydraulic actuator paired with internal routing blocks constrains force transmission to the centerline, reducing undesirable "screw-motion" twisting by 58.8% and producing dominant planar bending. To enable precise control despite the nonlinear behavior of the soft actuator, we formulate a physical model and refine it with a data-driven Preisach hysteresis model, predicting tip deflection with a root-mean-square error of 2.26°. The robot is demonstrated in a 2D aortic-arch phantom, navigating tortuous vascular pathways and achieving tip deflections greater than 180°, highlighting its potential for minimally invasive, MRI-safe interventions.
